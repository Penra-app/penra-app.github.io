Best offline, on-device AI note app for Android

If you want a notes tool whose AI (handwriting and PDF reading, voice to text, search by meaning, questions over your notes) runs on the phone with no account, no cloud and no internet after a one-time model download, Penra private notes for Android is the option to try; its core features are free with no usage limits. Know the limits before you install it: it is Android-only and new, sync between devices is not available yet (it is coming soon as a Pro feature), and it works best with 6 GB of RAM or more. This is a shortlist for choosing: for typed text, a Markdown vault or a meeting bot, another app below fits better.

What "offline" and "on-device AI" mean here

For this shortlist, an app counts as offline with on-device AI only if, after any one-time model download, handwriting reading, voice to text, search and questions over your notes keep working in airplane mode, with no account and without your notes being sent to a vendor server.

That rules out two kinds of app that often show up for the same search: the text-only offline notepad, which works without a connection but has no AI to read a page or answer a question, and the cloud AI notes tool, where you can type offline but transcription or "chat with your notes" runs on the vendor's servers.

Best if you want on-device AI with no account: Penra private notes for Android

There is no sign-up and no Penra server; the internet is used only when you choose to download the AI models. After that, on the phone:

  • Handwriting and PDFs to text: turn a photographed page or a PDF into editable text, and write in ink on PDFs and photos. More detail: how to turn handwriting into text offline.
  • Voice to text: on-device transcription, free with no usage limits. Speaker separation is a Pro feature. See the on-device alternative to Otter.
  • Find by meaning: search for what a note is about, not only the exact words in it.
  • Ask your notes: Ask in your own words. Penra writes a short answer from your notes and shows which note it came from; if it's not in your notes, it won't make it up. More: private second brain app.

Honest limits

  • Android only, and new. Windows and iOS versions are coming but are not available yet.
  • No sync yet. Sync between devices is coming soon as a Pro feature; if you need the same notes on several devices today, pick another tool for now.

Closest on-device AI alternative: SoloNote

SoloNote is what current answers for "offline AI notes" most often recommend, and it deserves the mention. Its own site says: no account and no servers, free with no subscription or ads, iPhone (iOS 17+) and Android 10+ (64-bit), a model that ships with the app (about 1.3 GB on Android), and 6 GB of RAM for AI on Android. It answers questions over your notes on the device and shows which notes it used.

Differences the vendor states: some features (Capture, Today, Spending) are iPhone-only, and on Android a PDF or photo you attach in Chat is read for that conversation rather than saved as a note. Map lookups and optional web search on Android leave the device. For a free, no-account assistant on iPhone and Android, it is a fair pick.

If you want a small, search-first Android notes tool: Uteke

Uteke (mobile.uteke.app; not Uteke Cloud) is an Android notes app with no account and no cloud sync. After a one-time search-model download of about 188 MB you can find notes by meaning. Its official Free plan, checked 2026-11-11, caps you at 250 notes, 5 collections, 10 documents and 5 voice dictations a day; Pro is $4.99 a month or $49.99 a year (USD, billed through Google Play; storefront prices may differ). The official site does not advertise photographed handwriting-to-text or PDF reading as a core capture path. Full comparison: A Uteke alternative for Android.

If you want a mature handwriting notebook: Notability (or XNote for pen and paper)

Notability is the more established handwriting app. Its FAQ and pricing pages list stylus handwriting, PDF markup, audio and handwriting recognition, with transcription and "chat with your notes" on paid plans, on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Android and the web. Starter is free with 5 notes. Sync and collaboration run through Notability Cloud, with an account. Where Notability's AI processing runs is not stated on the pages we checked, so we do not label it either cloud or on-device. More: a Notability alternative for Android that works offline.

XNote (xnote.ai) is a smart pen and notebook, XNote Write, at $199. You write in ink on XNote paper and the app turns pages into text you can search and ask questions about. It needs a sign-in, and XNote says pages and recordings are processed in the cloud. The free plan includes unlimited handwriting-to-text, AI chat with usage limits and 150 audio minutes a month. Full comparison: XNote smart pen alternative for Android. (Not the unrelated open-source XNotes app.)

If you want local Markdown: Obsidian

Obsidian's pricing page says no sign-up is required, notes are stored locally, and it is free for personal use; Sync is an optional, end-to-end encrypted paid add-on ($4 per user per month, billed annually). It is a strong, mature choice for linked Markdown notes, but has no built-in handwriting reading or on-device question answering; local language-model setups are separate plugins you assemble yourself. See the full comparison.

If you only need encrypted text that never goes online: Scrib

Scrib's own site describes no account, no cloud, AES-256 encryption and an Android version without the internet permission; it is free with no ads or tracking, on iPhone, Android and a Windows desktop editor. It has no handwriting AI, no ask-your-notes and, by design, no sync. Many "best offline notes" lists pick it, fairly, for typed private notes.

If you need a bot in Zoom or Meet: Otter, not an on-device recorder

Otter is an account-based cloud transcription service. Its privacy page says data is stored with AWS S3 and describes training on de-identified audio and transcripts; its pricing page lists a Basic plan with 300 monthly transcription minutes, paid plans above that, and a bot that joins Zoom, Teams and Google Meet. For team meeting notes, Otter fits. Penra private notes cannot join calls; it records through the phone's microphone. More: Otter alternative that transcribes on your device.

Will it run on your phone?

The published numbers for Penra private notes for Android:

  • RAM: 6 GB or more recommended; about 8 GB for the largest reading model.
  • First download: about 4 GB of AI models, once.
  • Speed: about 30 to 60 seconds for a full handwritten page on a mid-range Android phone.
  • Tested on (owner's testing): Samsung Galaxy S24 FE, realme GT 2 Pro, Samsung Galaxy A34.

This is a category-wide constraint: SoloNote also states 6 GB of RAM for AI on Android.

How to check that notes are not being sent

You do not have to take a privacy claim on trust. After the one-time model download, turn on airplane mode and use search, transcription and ask-your-notes: if they keep working, the AI runs on the phone. For a stricter check, compare Android's own data-usage counters for the app before and after a session. The step-by-step method and our own measurement are on the verify it yourself page.
